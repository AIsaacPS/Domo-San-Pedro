Abstract
Geographical Information System (GIS) is used to determine the spatial association
between geophysical and geological evidence and production zones in a well-known
geothermal Æeld (Los Azufres, Mexico). Surface observations in Los Azufres were used to
delineate areas characterized by high permeability and hot Øuid transport from the
reservoir: main faults, superÆcial fracture density, surface manifestations, contacts with the
most recent rhyolite domes, and low values in the apparent resistivity surveys. Three
knowledge-driven models were constructed based on a conceptual model of the Æeld: a
hydrothermal system in rough terrain with secondary permeability. Boolean, Index Overlay
and Fuzzy scheme models were proposed and the results obtained show a good correlation
with the location of the producing and non-producing wells that have been drilled in the
Æeld. The results obtained are useful for well siting (Boolean and Low-Risk Fuzzy models)
or  for  planning  further  detailed  exploration  (Index  Overlay  and  High-Risk  Fuzzy
models).#2000 CNR. Published by Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

GIS models have been successfully applied in regional exploration studies of

formation of mineral deposits are too complex to permit the application of
mathematical analytical models; therefore, the knowledge of an expert is required
to provide the proper weighing of the available evidence and their integration in a
predictive map of mineral deposits potential (Bonham-Carter, 1991).
Geothermal  exploration  could  use  this  kind  of  technique  after  the
reconnaissance exploration stage, when enough data have been obtained to decide
about further detailed exploration or well drilling. The accomplishment of drilling
a discovery well is the end of the exploration stage and the start of exploitation.
Before a well can be sited, the collection of geoscientiÆc data usually includes
surface geology, resistivity surveys, geomagnetic and gravity measurements, and
geochemistry of surface manifestations. These data must be analyzed by experts
who will take a decision about the best location for siting a discovery well. The
complexity of active hydrothermal systems is similar to the processes that generate
mineral deposits in whose exploration GISs have proved to be eective. The
application of a GIS to mineral exploration has always been done in a regional
setting; however, in the case that is presented here, the analysis is focused on the
potential of a GIS to predict the location of the most productive areas within a
single geothermal Æeld. The application of a GIS is a further step in the evolution
of computer-aided decision-taking processes in geothermal exploration. A well-
known Æeld (Los Azufres, Mexico) was used to estimate spatial relationships
among all the available and relevant thematic maps and the geothermal reservoir.
The Los Azufres geothermal Æeld is already producing electricity from geothermal
energy and the location of the producing areas is known well enough to perform a
comparison of the results of the model and the proved occurrence of production
areas.
2. Geological setting
Los Azufres geothermal Æeld is located in the central part of the Mexican
Volcanic Belt. The geologic studies indicate that it is contained in a complex
caldera structure (Fig. 1). Outcrop samples show that silicic volcanic rocks are the
most common in the area, and that ignimbrite layers and rhyolitic domes
predominate; the regional stratigraphy of Los Azufres area has been described by
Ferrari et al. (1991). Cores and cuttings from the wells drilled in the geothermal

due to secondary porosity, so that the production areas are constrained to main
active faults. Regional structures are characterized by an extension system that

3. Geophysical data
Geophysical studies performed in the Æeld have included resistivity surveys,
gravimetric and magnetometric maps (Garcı

to deÆne the location of intrusive bodies and regional faults. In the Los Azufres
case, geomagnetic and gravity maps do not show sharp anomalies within the Æeld,
as they are dominated by regional trends. Gravity data indicate that Los Azufres
geothermal Æeld is located in a gravity minimum anomaly that may be related
with a depression Ælled by volcanic-sedimentary strata. The local maxima in
gravimetric and magnetometric maps are interpreted as being produced by

The best indicators of the production zones are the low apparent resistivity

clays) and rock saturated with high-temperature saline Øuids. CFE (Comision
Federal de Electricidad) performed resistivity measurements with a Schlumberger

may be misleading due to the fact that low resistivity may be caused by the
presence of hot saline water and/or clay minerals. In the case of clay minerals,
they can also be present in areas of relic hydrothermal activity where, at present,
no geothermal potential might be available. In order to distinguish the currently
active areas from the relic hydrothermal areas at depth, information is needed on
the geothermal gradient, which was not available for Los Azufres. The analysis
was therefore undertaken assuming that all areas with low resistivity were
prospective for geothermal exploitation.
4. Conceptual model of the Æeld
A model of the resource to be studied is essential in order to judge the utility of
the geoscientiÆc data to provide the best information, in this case, about the
geothermal  production  areas.  From  the  evidence  provided  by  surface
manifestations chemistry and the geological data, the Los Azufres geothermal Æeld
can be considered as a ``typical'' rough topography hydrothermal system, where
the manifestations on the main upØow of the system are acid-sulfate hot springs
(steam-heated water) and fumaroles (Huitron et al., 1991). The rough topography
favors ascent of vapor and gases derived from boiling of the geothermal Øuid at
depth. The mixture of vapor and gases condenses close to the surface, generating
acid hot springs (due to H
2
S and CO
2
oxidation). In some areas the Øuids emerge
in the form of fumaroles. In this type of geothermal Æeld, there might be a spatial
relationship between the acid surface manifestations and the location of the
reservoir at depth.
The stratigraphic column in the area shows the predominance of volcanic rocks
and the lack of porous strata with high primary permeability (Ferrari et al., 1991;
Huitron et al., 1991), as would be the case for Cerro Prieto (Pelayo et al., 1991),
which indicates that the reservoir permeability must be related to fracturing. This
fact increases the importance of including a thematic map with all the main faults.
The latter can be mapped by surface studies, and their importance at depth can
only be estimated from their relation with the geophysical data that provide
information on the resistivity of the deep strata. As some faults may have strayed
at depth, the integration with resistivity data would provide a way to dierentiate
the sealed faults from those in the active areas. The apparent resistivity map used
in this study was constructed from data obtained with a Schlumberger array with
a 2 km separation between electrodes (AB/2=1 km). This electrode separation

6. GIS and data integration modeling
In applying data integration models  to exploration  of natural  resources,
expertise is important in the selection of the maps that will provide predictor keys
of the resource to be studied, and if possible to avoid the cost of gathering
redundant information. Also, the assignment of weights (in the Index Overlay
model) and the relative importance of the evidence (in the Fuzzy data analysis)
follow empirical relationships that depend on the conceptual model of the system
to be studied. Firstly, the selection should be made between data-driven and
knowledge-driven  models. Data-driven  models  process  the input  data using
weights of evidence, logistic regression or neural network analysis, and require
training data to calculate the model parameters. Knowledge-driven models include
model parameters that are estimated by an expert using fuzzy data analysis, or

smaller than the estimation of the favorable distance to faults used in mineral

is not the case of Los Azufres, where the main faults have large dip angles
(Huitron et al., 1991). Transport of the Øuid from deeper parts of the reservoir to
the surface is evidenced by surface manifestations such as hot springs, fumaroles
and hot pools. Surface observations in Los Azufres can be used to delineate areas
characterized by high permeability and hot Øuid transport from the reservoir.
These areas can be outlined in the resulting image after the proper combination of
the thematic maps that contain the main faults, the fractured areas, the surface
manifestations and the boundaries of the most recent rhyolitic domes. Conditions
at  depth  related  with  producing  zones  are  characterized  by  low  apparent
resistivity, typically less than 10 ohm m. The simultaneous occurrence of low

evidence in each element by the weight and the order weight. The order weight is
assigned to the evidence in relation with the degree of risk that may be taken in
the decision process; in the case of a low-risk decision, a high order weight must
be assigned to the evidence with higher weight in the pair-wise comparison
process. The result is the same as that of applying the Fuzzy AND operator,
which minimizes the favorability value assigned to each area element; the operator
considers each evidence as necessary but not suÅcient to yield a high favorability
value. When a high-risk decision is acceptable, a high order weight is assigned to
evidence with a low pair-wise comparison weight; this calculates the Fuzzy OR
operator that maximizes the result. This operator considers any evidence as
suÅcient and will therefore facilitate the inclusion of an area element as favorable
in the resulting favorability map.
The order weights may be varied with the restriction that their sum must not be
greater than one. Where the order weights have the same value for each evidence
map, the result is a weighted linear combination of the evidence.
Here  we  present  only  the  results  obtained  with  the  high-  and  low-risk
combination of ordered weights, as they show the results obtained in both
extremes of the Multi-Criteria Evaluation. The results are presented in Figs. 10
and 11.
The low-risk image is similar to the result of the Boolean analysis; many
producing wells are left out of the areas marked as favorable, and none of the
non-producing wells are included. This shows that the criteria and relative weights
assigned in the fuzzy analysis are concordant with the conditions (1±5) established
in the GIS model. The high-risk image includes as favorable all pixels that satisfy
any restrictive criteria; it can, therefore be noted that although all producing wells
are contained in the marked areas, the non-producing wells are also included.
7. Discussion
In mineral exploration as well as in geothermal exploration, the emphasis is laid
on structural and geologic features, and on geochemical data (Campbell et al.,
1982;  Agterberg,  1989;  Bonham-Carter  et  al.,  1988;  Chung  et  al.,  1992;

McCammon, 1993; Bonham-Carter et al., 1994). In both cases, the key evidence
used is similar, so that the digital integration framework of geoscientiÆc data
developed in the mineral industry can be adapted to geothermal resources
exploration.
In geothermal exploration, the areas to be identiÆed are those in which
geothermal  Øuids  can  be  found  at  temperatures  higher  than  2008C,  and
permeability is high enough to allow the inØow of geothermal Øuid from the
reservoir to the vicinity of the well. The requirements for those areas are given in
the inference network (Fig. 6) presented here for a geothermal Æeld located in
rough terrain and with permeability parameters related to fracturing of the
reservoir rock.
The similarity of the results obtained with the Boolean and the Low-Risk
models shows that the inference network used as the basis of the model and the
relative importance of the evidence used in the assignment of the weights in the
Multi-Criteria Evaluation are in good agreement.

proved to be too restrictive, as the areas marked as favorable are those in which
all criteria in the inference network are satisÆed. As a result of these restrictions,
many production wells were left out of the favorable areas. It must, therefore be

The Index Overlay and the High-Risk models lessen the requirements for each

wells. This model can provide helpful data for planning further drilling, and can
also be used to plan detailed exploration. The High-Risk model delineates a large
area that includes non-producing wells and can therefore only be used to plan
further detailed pre-drilling.
Frequently, production wells are located within the low resistivity anomalies, at
the  intersection  of  main  faults  and  at  a  short  distance  from  the  surface
manifestations. However, the results show that more areas may be suitable for
geothermal exploitation, and a GIS model should be constructed to take into
account all the evidence, in order to deÆne all areas likely to be exploited without
overlooking areas with an important geothermal potential. The integration of all
the available data in a GIS provides an important tool for the evaluation of
geothermal Æelds in dierent exploration and development stages.
8. Conclusions
In  the  early  exploration  stages  of  areas  where  secondary  permeability
predominates, preliminary siting of exploration wells can be achieved by means of
a geographical information system based on surface geology and resistivity
surveys. This system can also be used to identify areas suited to more detailed
exploration before drilling an expensive exploration well; for instance, costly
detailed geothermal gradient measurements could be performed only in the areas
that the Index Overlay model has assigned favorability values higher than 0.6.
In Los Azufres a good agreement was obtained between the location of the
production wells and the areas delineated by Boolean, Index Overlay and Fuzzy
data analysis models on the basis of available geoscientiÆc data.
